# Product principles

One product, one voice, from the website to every screen in the app. Every
design and every UX review starts here.

## Simple first, even when that means opinionated

Services are run by volunteers. Every option we add is a decision someone has
to make on a Sunday morning, so we would rather be slightly restrictive and
obvious than limitlessly flexible.

- **Fewer concepts.** Reuse what exists — the library, the service (setlist),
  the song text format, the display look — before inventing a new noun, page
  or mode.
- **One way to do a thing.** No parallel paths to the same result.
- **Fixed, good defaults over settings.** A setting needs a reason a volunteer
  would change it. Detail goes in a one-line hint and an (i) tip, not more
  controls.
- **Automate what can be automated.** The app picks the screen setup, splits
  slides, follows the room. People confirm; they don't configure.
- **Hide it until it's used.** Power features live behind "⋯" or appear once
  they're active, never as permanent header buttons.

## Where we stop

We put the words a room needs to read on the screen, in the church's look, and
the AI keeps up. We do not author slides.

**If it needs a canvas, it's out**: free-positioned text, shapes, per-slide
fonts, transitions, layers, timeline cues. When someone needs a designed
slide, they design it elsewhere and bring it in — the answer is a restriction
plus an escape hatch, not a feature.

## Reviewing against this

A UX review asks, for each screen: what can be removed, merged into an
existing concept, made a default, or automated? A finding that a screen is
"missing an option" is only valid if the option can't be a better default.
