Privacy

Last updated 24 November 2026

Church Slides is made by Martin Main, an individual developer in Nova Scotia, Canada. This page explains what the app and this website send, where it goes, and how to turn it off. Martin is responsible for privacy here; questions, or a request to see or delete your data: martinmain93@gmail.com.

  • Your songs, setlists, logs and any service recordings stay on your computer. The exceptions are the short log extract in an error report, and anything your church chooses to share through a Church Slides account (see below).
  • Audio from the microphone or sound desk is never sent to us.
  • The app doesn’t record services unless you turn recording on.
  • No advertising, no selling of data, and no cookies or tracking on this website.
  • The app asks before it sends us anything automatically, and you can change your mind in Settings → Privacy.

Who is responsible for what

For error reports, feedback, usage statistics and download counts, we decide what is collected and why, so we are responsible for them. Everything else (your library, setlists, settings, the app’s log, and any recordings) is held only on your church’s computers. Your church is responsible for that, and we don’t receive it, apart from the log extract in an error report.

If your church uses a Church Slides account, we also hold your account details, for which we are responsible, and what your church shares through its organization (its live set, and its library once that’s shared). That content is your church’s: it decides what goes in and who sees it, and we store it and pass it on for the church.

What the app sends

Error reports

When something goes wrong, the app sends a technical report so it can be fixed: the error, the app’s version, the computer’s model, system version and chip, and the end of the app’s log. The log can include song titles, lyrics and audio device names. Before a report leaves your computer, the app is designed to remove anything said into the microphone, your user name, the computer’s name, and people’s names in device names (such as “Sarah’s AirPods”). Reports carry a random install number that doesn’t say who you are, plus a contact only if you type one in.

The app asks when you first open it, with error reports ticked; you can untick it then, or turn it off any time in Settings → Privacy → Send error reports. Nothing is sent before you’ve answered.

Usage statistics

Only if you say yes when the app asks. For each service: how long it ran, how many songs it had, how long the AI was on, how many slide changes were made by the AI and how many by hand, and which screen layout was used. Once a day: how many songs are in the library and whether the AI could start. Never song titles, lyrics, setlist names, or anything typed or said.

With usage statistics on, the app’s update checks (when it opens, then once a day) also send a random install number, the app’s version, and the computer’s system version and chip, so we know how many installs are in use and which systems need supporting. Our server notes the country a request comes from, but doesn’t keep your IP address. With it off, update checks still happen but aren’t counted.

The install number isn’t your name or your church’s, but because it stays the same it is still treated as personal data, and everything on this page applies to it.

Off unless you agree. Change it any time: Settings → Privacy → Share usage statistics.

Feedback

When you use Send feedback, we get your message, the email or church name you give (if any), the install number, the app version and system details. For a problem report you can also attach the app’s recent log, cleaned as described under error reports. Feedback is also delivered to the developer’s email so we can reply.

Service recordings

The app can save a recording of each service (the audio, a transcript of what the AI heard, and when each slide changed) to help tune the AI. It is off unless someone turns it on in Settings → Developer tools, and the app explains what it captures before it starts. While it’s on, the operator screen shows Recording. Recordings stay on your computer, are never sent to us, and are deleted after 30 days unless you choose otherwise or approve one in Developer tools to keep.

A recording picks up everything the microphone hears, which can include prayers, announcements, visitors and children. If your church records, it is responsible for telling the people present (for example on the service sheet or a notice at the door) and for following the recording and privacy laws where you are.

The app turns speech into text to follow the songs. It doesn’t identify who is speaking or create voiceprints.

Features that go online because you choose them

These go to other companies, not to us, and each is used only if you set it up. Where your church uses them, your church is that company’s customer and is responsible for telling the people present if live audio leaves the building.

  • Speech model download. The AI’s speech model is downloaded once from Hugging Face, which sees your computer’s internet address.
  • Adding hymns. Library → Add hymns fetches hymns from the Open Hymnal Project (openhymnal.org), which sees your computer’s internet address.
  • Online speech or AI services. If you enter your own API key for an online service (such as Groq), live audio or the text the AI heard goes to that service under your account and its terms. So, in the song editor, do the words and chords of the song you’re charting when you ask for chord suggestions, and your recording when you use Voice. Keys are stored unencrypted in the app’s folder on your computer.
  • AI assistants. If you connect Claude or another assistant to your library (this computer’s, or your organization’s through our server), the songs and setlists it reads go to that assistant’s provider under your own account. Changes it makes to an organization’s library are recorded with your name and the assistant’s, like your own changes.
  • Online band screens (your own relay). These run in your church’s own Cloudflare account, not ours.
  • Band screens and setlist sharing on your network. These talk directly between computers on your church’s network and don’t go through us.

Church Slides accounts (preview)

Accounts are new and being tried with a few churches. You don’t need one: without one, nothing here applies. A church that uses one creates an organization and invites its team.

  • Your account. When you sign in (with Google), we keep your email address, your name as Google gives it, and Google’s identifier for your account, so the same person is recognized next time. Google sees that you signed in to Church Slides. For each computer or phone you’re signed in on, we keep when it was last used, so you can sign it out.
  • Your organization. Its name and time zone, who is in it and their role, the invites and band links made for it (each band link has the name it was given), and a record of what was done and by whom (for example, who invited someone or changed a role), so a problem can be traced.
  • The live set. When a service goes live through your organization, the service passes through our server to your band’s screens: the songs’ words and chords, the service order and which slide is showing. It’s kept for up to six hours after the last screen disconnects.
  • A shared library (when your church turns it on). Your organization’s songs, chords and keys, services, the record of what was sung when, the display look, and the timing the AI learned from your services are kept on our server so every computer in the team has them. When two people change a song’s words at once, the earlier words are kept too, with the name of the person whose change replaced them.
  • The web app (app.churchslides.app). Signed in there, a browser keeps a copy of your organization’s library and your sign-in, so it opens quickly and works for a while offline. Signing out on that device removes both, and if someone else signs in on that browser they start with a fresh copy. Nothing else is kept there except whether you like chords shown, and the web app sends us nothing beyond signing in and the changes you make.
  • AI through your organization. A computer signed in to an organization can use its AI allowance instead of an AI account of its own, for spoken requests, finding Bible verses, chord suggestions and online speech. Then the audio or text for each request goes through our server to Groq, which runs the AI for us, and the answer comes back the same way. We don’t keep the audio, the text or the answers: only how much was used each day (requests and seconds of audio), for a week. A key of your own, if you enter one, is used instead.

Everyone in an organization can see its library, its live set, and who is in it (names and roles); its owner and admins also see members’ email addresses. We don’t look at your church’s content except where needed to keep the service running or when you ask us for help, and we never use it for anything else.

An organization’s owner can ask us to delete it, which removes its library, live set and records from our server. To delete your account, email us; we’ll remove it and your sign-ins, and reply within 30 days. Songs you added to an organization stay with that organization.

This website and downloads

The download buttons pass through our server, which counts each download: which installer, the country it came from, whether it was a Mac or Windows browser, and the site that linked to us. It doesn’t keep IP addresses. The installers and updates themselves come from GitHub, which sees your internet address as any website does. The site sets no cookies and runs no analytics scripts. Our host, Netlify, keeps standard web server logs.

Where it goes, and for how long

  • Error reports and feedback: Sentry, in the United States, kept for Sentry’s standard retention period.
  • Feedback emails: the developer’s email (Google), kept as long as it’s useful for helping you; ask and we’ll delete it.
  • Usage statistics and download counts: our own server on Cloudflare, under Cloudflare’s data processing terms. Kept for up to two years, then deleted automatically.
  • Accounts, organizations, live sets and shared libraries: our own server on Cloudflare, under the same terms. Kept while the account or organization exists, except the live set (up to six hours after a service).
  • AI through your organization: passed to Groq, in the United States, which runs it for us under its terms. We keep only daily usage counts, for a week.

This means some data is stored outside Canada, where it can be accessed by courts and authorities under that country’s laws.

Only people working on Church Slides have access to this data. Sentry, Google and Cloudflare store it for us under their data processing terms and don’t use it for themselves; Groq runs AI requests for us under its terms. It’s used to fix problems and decide what to build next, and never for anything else.

Your choices and rights

Each kind of automatic sending has its own switch in Settings → Privacy, and changes take effect straight away. Error reports are on unless you turn them off, after the app has told you about them; usage statistics are sent only if you say yes. You can change either at any time. Feedback is sent because you choose to send it. Download counts hold nothing that identifies you.

You can ask to see or delete what we hold about your install: use Ask for my data to be deleted at the bottom of Settings → Privacy, or email us your install number (shown in the same place). We’ll delete what we can find linked to that number and reply within 30 days. Standard server logs kept by our hosts can’t be searched by install number.

If you’re not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, or in Quebec, the Commission d’accès à l’information. If you’re in the UK or EU, you can also complain to your data protection authority.

Children

Church Slides is made for church teams, not for children, and we don’t knowingly collect anything from children.
